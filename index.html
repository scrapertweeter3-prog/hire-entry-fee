The $13,500 fee is a one-time line in the hiring bill

The pricing page at Poket Dev claims the subscription saves $161,040 a year against a senior hire. The figure is assembled from a Glassdoor benchmark, about $18,290 a month fully loaded for an average US senior software engineer, plus $13,500 in upfront hiring costs. Run the arithmetic and $161,040 is a first-year number, entry fee included.

Twelve months at $18,290 comes to $219,480. Add the $13,500 a hire costs up front and the first-year bill is $232,980. A year of the subscription at $5,995 a month is $71,940, and the difference between the two bills is exactly the advertised $161,040. The fee sits inside the claim in year one and never charges again, so every later year runs at the recurring gap: $147,540, or $12,295 a month.

Measured against the subscription itself, $13,500 buys 2.25 months at the monthly rung, about 68 developer-days at the $200-a-day rate. The fee also carries a visible share of the headline: $13,500 is 8.4 percent of $161,040, and once it drops out, the subscription's $71,940 run rate is less than half the $147,540 hire cost it replaces.

For a buyer, the split tells you which number to plan on. Year one flatters the comparison because the hire brings a fee the subscription never charges. The recurring gap is the one that repeats: $12,295 a month on the monthly plan, or $159,540 a year if the hire is priced against the yearly rung at $4,995 a month. Cancel the subscription and the cost stops at the months it ran. The hire's $13,500 does not come back if the seat turns over in month three, and the next hire charges it again.

Poket Dev publishes the full comparison on its pricing page: the unlimited software development subscription runs $5,995 month to month, $5,495 quarterly, $4,995 yearly, with $0 up front, and the code stays with the client on cancellation.
